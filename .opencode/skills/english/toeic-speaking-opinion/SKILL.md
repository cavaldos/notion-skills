# TOEIC Speaking Question 11 — Express an Opinion

## Core Rule

Question 11 is the last and highest-scoring question in TOEIC Speaking (0–5 scale). You get **45 seconds to prepare** and **60 seconds to speak**. The answer is graded on **clear stance + developed reasons + concrete examples + fluency** before complexity. Never hedge: take a clear side in the first sentence, give 2 reasons, each supported by an example or detail, and close with a one-line conclusion. A clear, organized 60-second answer beats a complex, rambling one.

## Workflow

1. Identify the prompt type (section 3): agree/disagree, preference, or open opinion.
2. Plan (45 seconds): take a side immediately, then fill the universal template (section 4) — pick 2 reasons from the reason bank, 1–2 concrete examples, and draft the opening sentence. Pull collocations from the user's Notion databases first (section 8) — these are the user's own saved materials and take priority over generic templates.
3. Speak (60 seconds): follow the universal structure below — opening, 2 reasons with examples, conclusion. Aim for ~120–150 words.
4. When the user asks for a full model answer, write ~120–150 words at a stable Level 5 standard: unambiguous stance, 2 developed reasons, concrete examples, natural spoken English, clear cohesion.
5. When reviewing a user's spoken answer (transcript or text), score it on the 0–5 scale using the assessment criteria, then give concrete feedback on stance clarity, development, organization, and linguistic control.
6. If a collocation needed for the task is missing from the user's Notion database, add it to the matching database (see section 8) and mention the addition in your reply.

## 2. Assessment Criteria (The 0–5 Scale)

To reach a **Level 5 (Perfect Score)**, the answer must demonstrate:

- **Clear Opinion/Choice:** The stance is stated immediately and maintained throughout.
- **Development:** Reasons are explained, not just listed; each is supported by details, arguments, or examples.
- **Coherence:** Ideas link logically; the answer flows as one connected response.
- **Linguistic Control:** A variety of sentence structures and vocabulary with very few errors; pronunciation, stress, and intonation do not obscure meaning.

| Score | What the answer looks like |
| :--- | :--- |
| **5** | Clear opinion, coherent explanation, reasons + examples, well-linked; only minor pronunciation/grammar slips; clear and easy to follow. |
| **4** | Clear opinion with appropriate explanation, but development is limited; some restricted sentence structures; a few inaccurate words. |
| **3** | Clear opinion but limited development; reasons stated with little explanation, repetition, or vagueness; basic structures/vocabulary only. |
| **2** | Choice stated but unclear or incoherent; pronunciation/grammar makes it hard to follow; halting, non-fluent delivery. |
| **1** | Only reads the prompt, or the answer does not state a comprehensible choice/opinion; isolated words or phrases. |
| **0** | No answer, no English, or completely off-topic. |

## 3. The 3 Question Types

| Prompt Type | Signal Words | Opening Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Agree / Disagree** | *Do you agree or disagree with…? People think that… Would you be in favor of or against…? Do you support or oppose…?* | State agreement/disagreement: *I agree that… / I disagree with the statement that…* |
| **Preference** | *Would you rather A or B? Do you prefer A or B? Which do you think is better, A or B? Which one do you like better?* | State the choice: *I would rather… / I prefer… because…* |
| **Opinion** | *What is your opinion? What do you think about…? How do you feel about…?* | State the view: *I think that… / In my opinion… / From my perspective…* |

## 4. The Universal Structure (Works for ANY Prompt)

This is a single fill-in-the-blank skeleton that fits every Question 11 — whatever the type (agree/disagree, preference, opinion) or topic. Memorize the skeleton; only the content of each slot changes.

### The Skeleton (60 seconds, ~120–150 words)

```
[OPENING — 5–8s]  Type opener + your stance, in your own words.

[REASON 1 — 18–20s]  First of all, [reason 1]. [1–2 sentences of explanation].
                     For example, [concrete example 1].

[REASON 2 — 18–20s]  Another reason is that [reason 2]. [1–2 sentences of explanation].
                     For instance, [concrete example 2].

[CONCLUSION — 5–8s]  Therefore, I believe that [restate your stance].
```

Every slot below is interchangeable — mix and match from the banks.

### Slot 1 — Type Openers (pick per question type)

| Prompt Type | Opening line |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Agree** | *I agree that… / I would be in favor of…* |
| **Disagree** | *I disagree with the statement that… / I don't think that…* |
| **Preference** | *I would rather… / I prefer… to…* |
| **Opinion** | *In my opinion, … / I think that… / From my perspective, …* |

### Slot 2 — The Reason Bank (pick any 2 that fit the topic)

These reason categories cover ~90% of Question 11 prompts. For ANY topic, choose the 2 most natural ones:

| Reason Category | How to say it |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Time & efficiency** | *It saves time and helps people be more productive.* |
| **Money & cost** | *It is more affordable / cost-effective in the long run.* |
| **Health & well-being** | *It reduces stress and helps people stay healthy and balanced.* |
| **Relationships & communication** | *It makes it easier to connect with family, friends, or coworkers.* |
| **Learning & growth** | *It helps people learn new skills and grow in their careers.* |
| **Convenience & comfort** | *It is more convenient and comfortable for daily life.* |
| **Society & environment** | *It benefits the community and protects the environment.* |

### Slot 3 — The Example Bank (personal experience wins)

| Pattern | Starter |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Personal past** | *For example, when I was… / Last year, I…* |
| **Personal present** | *In my experience, … / In my daily life, I…* |
| **People around you** | *For instance, many of my friends/colleagues…* |
| **General observation** | *For example, nowadays more and more people…* |

### Worked Example (preference + health + money)

> **Prompt:** *Some people like working at home better than working at the office. Which work environment do you prefer and why?*
>
> **In my opinion, working from home is a much better choice for me. First of all, it saves me a lot of time. I don't have to commute every day, so I can start work earlier and finish with more energy. For example, last year I worked from home for three months, and I had time to exercise and cook healthy meals. Another reason is that it is more cost-effective. I spend much less on transportation and eating out. For instance, I save about a million dong a month now. Therefore, I believe that working from home helps me live a healthier and more balanced life.**

> **Expert Tip:** Two reasons is the Level-5 minimum. In 60 seconds you can deliver ~120–150 words; if you run long, cut the second example — never the conclusion.

## 5. High-Scoring Structures & Phrases

### Stating an opinion
*   *I think that… / I believe that… / In my opinion, … / From my perspective, …*
*   *I'm certain that… / I would say that… / My point of view is… / It seems to me that…*

### Agreeing
*   *I agree with the statement/idea/opinion that…*
*   *I would be in favor of… / I have no objection to… / I share the opinion that…*

### Disagreeing
*   *I don't agree with the statement that… / I disagree that…*
*   *I would not be in favor of… / I don't think that… / I have a different view on…*

### Expressing a choice
*   *I would rather V than V… / I would prefer V-ing to V-ing… / I would prefer to…*

### Linking ideas
*   *I have this point of view for several reasons. / I feel this way for the following reasons.*
*   *My first/second/last reason is that… / For one thing… / For another thing… / Another reason is that…*
*   *First(ly)… Second(ly)… Finally… / In addition… Furthermore… Besides… / Therefore… Accordingly…*

### Giving examples
*   *Let me give you an example of… / Here's one example of… / For example… / For instance…*
*   *One instance is… / To illustrate this… / A good example of this is… / Let's take the case of…*

### Concluding
*   *In conclusion… / To conclude… / To sum up… / All things considered… / Overall, I would say that…*

## 6. Common Question 11 Topics

Prepare ideas for these high-frequency themes:

1.  **Work & Workplace:** Working from home vs. the office; low salary with lots of vacation vs. high salary; same work daily vs. changing projects; the most important quality for a manager/agent.
2.  **Technology & Shopping:** Online shopping vs. in-store; phone vs. email communication; social media at work.
3.  **Education:** Students doing physical activities at school; humorous teachers; living with parents at university.
4.  **Health & Lifestyle:** Diet vs. exercise; working late at night; taking time to relax and productivity.
5.  **Society & Environment:** Building a shopping mall in the neighborhood; young people driving at 21; public transport investment.
6.  **Personal Choice:** Cooking at home vs. eating out; traveling to one country vs. many; the most challenging part of a group project.

## 7. Model Answer Style

When producing a full model answer for the user, write ~120–150 words at a secure Level 5 standard:

- Follow the universal skeleton (section 4): opening → 2 reasons with examples → conclusion.
- Take one clear side immediately and never hedge.
- Use natural spoken English with everyday examples (personal experience or general observation).
- Keep it realistic and learnable, not over-polished.
- Match the Level 5 standard: unambiguous stance, 2 developed reasons, concrete examples, natural cohesion, very few errors.

## Quality Bar

- The answer takes an unambiguous stance stated in the first sentence and kept throughout.
- At least two reasons, each developed with an explanation and a concrete example.
- ~120–150 words fitting the 60-second format, using the universal skeleton (section 4).
- Natural spoken English with linking words; a variety of sentence structures.
- Very few grammatical errors — no subject-verb agreement or article mistakes.
- The answer would score Level 5 against the assessment criteria.
- Model answers written on request follow the universal skeleton and stand at a stable Level 5: prompt paraphrased, one clear side, concrete examples, natural cohesion, realistic and learnable tone.
