# TOEIC Writing Essay (Question 8)

## Core Rule

TOEIC essays are graded on the 0-5 scale and judged by **quantity + clarity** before complexity. Never produce a neutral or hedging essay: take a clear side in the first paragraph and maintain it throughout. Every reason must be supported by a concrete example.

## Workflow

1. Identify the prompt and its type (agree/disagree, preference, or pros/cons opinion).
2. Plan (5 minutes): take a side immediately, list 2 reasons and 2 concrete examples, and draft a one-sentence thesis. Pull the structures and vocabulary from the user's Notion databases first (see section 8) — these are the user's own saved materials and take priority over generic templates.
3. Write (22 minutes): follow the 5-paragraph "Power Structure" below — introduction, 2 body paragraphs (topic sentence + explanation + example), conclusion. Aim for 300-350 words, using the user's saved structures and collocations from Notion where they fit. Develop the explanation inside each body paragraph with the DIF method (section 9) so ideas stay logically connected instead of feeling disjointed.
4. Polish (3 minutes): check subject-verb agreement (e.g., *He works* vs *He work*), article usage (*a/an/the*), spelling of common business words (*business, environment, opportunity*), and apply the writing style constraints in section 10.
5. When the user asks for a full model essay, follow the essay craft borrowed from the IELTS Task 2 teacher samples — paraphrase the prompt, one idea per paragraph, concrete examples, natural cohesion — and write at a stable Level 5 standard (300-350 words, unambiguous stance, business English, no em dashes, DIF-developed body paragraphs).
6. When reviewing a user's draft, score it on the 0-5 scale using the assessment criteria, then give concrete feedback on clarity, support, organization, and linguistic control.

## 1. The TOEIC Essay Blueprint

Unlike IELTS, the TOEIC essay is more focused on the **workplace** and gives you less time for a higher word count.

| Feature | Requirement | Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time Limit** | 30 Minutes | 5m Planning, 22m Writing, 3m Editing. |
| **Word Count** | 300-350 Words (Recommended) | Aim for 3-4 sentences per paragraph. |
| **Scoring** | 0 - 5 Scale | Focus on clarity, support, and organization. |
| **Context** | Business / General Social | Use professional but accessible language. |

---

## 2. Assessment Criteria (The 0-5 Scale)

To reach a **Level 5 (Perfect Score)**, your essay must demonstrate:

*   **Clarity of Opinion:** Your stance must be stated immediately and maintained throughout.
*   **Supportive Development:** Every reason must be backed by a specific example (personal experience or general observation).
*   **Organization:** Logical flow with clear paragraph transitions.
*   **Linguistic Control:** A variety of sentence structures with very few grammatical errors.

---

## 3. The 5-Paragraph "Power Structure"

Given the 30-minute limit, a standardized structure is your best friend.

### I. Introduction (40-50 words)
*   **Hook:** A general statement about the topic.
*   **Thesis Statement:** Your clear opinion (Agree/Disagree or Preference).
*   **Outline:** A brief mention of the two/three reasons you will discuss.

### II. Body Paragraph 1: First Reason (80-100 words)
*   **Topic Sentence:** State your first reason clearly.
*   **Explanation:** Elaborate on why this reason supports your opinion using the DIF method (section 9): Difference, then Implication, then For whom.
*   **Example:** Provide a concrete scenario (e.g., *"In my previous job at a marketing firm..."*).

### III. Body Paragraph 2: Second Reason (80-100 words)
*   **Topic Sentence:** State your second reason.
*   **Explanation:** Deepen the argument using the DIF method (section 9).
*   **Example:** Use a "What if" scenario or a general trend.

### IV. Body Paragraph 3: Optional Third Reason or Counter-Argument (Optional)
*   If time permits, add a third reason to ensure you hit the 300-word mark.

### V. Conclusion (40-50 words)
*   **Restate Thesis:** Use different words to state your opinion again.
*   **Summary:** Briefly recap the main points.
*   **Final Thought:** A closing recommendation or prediction.

---

## 4. TOEIC-Specific Vocabulary & Transitions

TOEIC favors **Business English** and **Logical Connectors**. Use these instead of an em dash to join or contrast ideas.

| Category | High-Scoring Phrases |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stating Opinion** | *In my view...*, *I strongly advocate for...*, *From my perspective...* |
| **Giving Reasons** | *First and foremost...*, *Another significant factor is...*, *Moreover...* |
| **Providing Examples** | *For instance...*, *To illustrate this point...*, *A prime example of this is...* |
| **Showing Contrast** | *On the other hand,...*, *Conversely,...*, *Despite these benefits,...*, *However,...* |
| **Concluding** | *In summary...*, *Ultimately...*, *Given these points, it is clear that...* |

## 5. Common TOEIC Essay Topics

Prepare ideas for these high-frequency themes:

1.  **Workplace Environment:** Working from home vs. in the office; Small vs. Large companies.
2.  **Management:** Qualities of a good leader; Teamwork vs. Individual work.
3.  **Technology:** Impact of social media on business; Automation in the workplace.
4.  **Personal Choices:** Spending money on travel vs. saving for the future; Importance of job satisfaction vs. salary.

---

## 6. Execution Checklist (The 30-Minute Sprint)

1.  **Minute 0-5 (Plan):** Choose a side immediately. Don't be neutral. List 2 reasons and 2 examples.
2.  **Minute 5-27 (Write):** Focus on getting your ideas down. Don't stop to find the "perfect" word. Do not reach for an em dash; use a comma, a period, or a connector from section 4 instead.
3.  **Minute 27-30 (Polish):** Check for "S-V Agreement" (e.g., *He works* vs *He work*), article usage (*a/an/the*), spelling of common business words (*business, environment, opportunity*), and scan for any em dash to remove per section 9.

> **Expert Tip:** In TOEIC, **Quantity + Clarity** often beats **Complexity**. It is better to write 320 words of clear, simple English than 200 words of complex, error-prone English.

## 9. DIF Method: Deepening a Point Without Breaking Coherence

A common way an essay loses coherence is jumping straight from a reason to an example, with no logical bridge connecting them. The **DIF method** fixes this by pushing each reason through three questions before moving to the example. Use it to write the "Explanation" sentence(s) inside each body paragraph (see section 3), so the paragraph deepens one idea step by step instead of listing disconnected points.

### D — Difference
- **Question to ask:** What difference does this make? What concrete change does this action or issue produce in reality?
- **Purpose:** State the direct, observable effect of the topic sentence before going further.

### I — Implication
- **Question to ask:** What is the implication? What is the longer-term consequence or deeper significance of that difference?
- **Purpose:** Push the reasoning one logical level deeper instead of stopping at the surface-level effect from D.

### F — For whom / What for
- **Question to ask:** Who is this (not) for? Which group of people or which situation does this solution or problem actually apply to?
- **Purpose:** Narrow the claim to a specific audience or context, which makes the argument more concrete and persuasive instead of a vague generalization.

### How to apply it inside a body paragraph

1. **Topic Sentence** — state the reason.
2. **D:** Add one sentence on the immediate, concrete difference this reason makes.
3. **I:** Add one sentence pushing that difference to its deeper implication.
4. **F:** Add one sentence (or fold it into the example) naming who this applies to or works best for.
5. **Example:** Close with a concrete scenario that illustrates the D-I-F chain just built.

This keeps the Explanation portion of each body paragraph (see section 3) logically connected sentence to sentence, rather than restating the topic sentence in different words or jumping straight to an unconnected example. Apply the writing style constraints in section 10 while writing the D, I, and F sentences: use standard punctuation and clear connectors (*this means that...*, *as a result...*, *this is especially true for...*) instead of an em dash to link the steps.

Not every paragraph needs all three steps spelled out as separate sentences, but default to splitting D, I, and F into their own sentences whenever each one carries a distinct idea. Only combine two steps into one sentence when they form a single continuous thought (for example, a difference stated together with its immediate cause). For shorter TOEIC paragraphs (80-100 words), F can be folded into the example sentence itself if it does not need its own claim. The goal is a clear, well-separated logical chain, not a rigid four-sentence template and not a crammed one either.

## 10. Writing Style Constraints (Always Apply)

The user does not want em dashes (—) anywhere in generated essays, outlines, or feedback, and wants writing that reads as clear and direct rather than stylistically ornate.

- **Never use an em dash (—) or en dash (–) as punctuation.** Do not substitute with `--` either.
- Replace what an em dash would normally do with the correct standard punctuation instead:
  - For a contrast or "but" idea: use a period + connector (*"...a degree. However, work..."*) or a comma + `but`/`yet`.
  - For an inserted aside: use commas, parentheses, or a separate sentence, not a dash.
  - For a dramatic pause or emphasis: use a colon or simply restructure into two sentences.
- Prefer short to medium sentences with one clear idea each over long sentences stitched together with dashes.
- **Split ideas clearly; do not cram multiple ideas into one sentence or one paragraph just to save space.** If a sentence is carrying two separate claims (e.g., a reason and its consequence, or two different examples), break it into two sentences. If a paragraph is covering two distinct points, split it into two paragraphs (using the optional third body paragraph in section 3 if needed) rather than forcing both points under one topic sentence.
- Only merge ideas into one sentence or paragraph when they are genuinely one continuous thought (e.g., a cause directly stated with its immediate effect). When in doubt, split rather than merge, since TOEIC raters reward organization and clarity over compactness.
- Avoid other overly literary flourishes that reduce clarity: rhetorical questions used as transitions, triple-adjective strings, and sentence fragments used for effect. TOEIC raters reward clear, well-organized, grammatically correct prose, not literary style.
- This constraint applies to every deliverable from this skill: model essays, outlines, brainstormed reasons/examples, and feedback comments on the user's own drafts.
- Before returning any essay text, scan it once for the — character and rewrite any sentence that contains one.

## Quality Bar

- The essay takes an unambiguous stance stated in the introduction and kept throughout.
- At least two reasons, each developed with an explanation and a concrete example.
- Each body paragraph's explanation is built with the DIF method (section 9): the reasoning moves from a concrete difference, to a deeper implication, to a specific audience or context, before landing on the example, rather than jumping straight from topic sentence to example.
- 300+ words using the 5-paragraph power structure within the 30-minute format.
- Professional business language with logical connectors; a variety of sentence structures.
- Very few grammatical errors — no subject-verb agreement or article mistakes.
- Ideas are split clearly across sentences and paragraphs rather than crammed together; each sentence carries one idea and each paragraph covers one point, splitting into an extra paragraph when a second distinct point appears.
- No em dashes anywhere in the text; contrast and asides are expressed with standard punctuation instead (see section 10).
- The essay would score Level 5 against the assessment criteria.
- Model essays written on request follow the essay craft and stand at a stable Level 5: prompt paraphrased, one clear side, DIF-developed body paragraphs, concrete examples, natural cohesion, realistic and learnable tone, and free of em dashes.
